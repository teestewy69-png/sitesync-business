A flip turns a house into a lump of cash. A BRRRR turns a house into a rental you own, ideally with most of your cash returned by a refinance. The choice is not about which strategy is better. It is about which one fits this house and your capital right now.

The cleanest way to decide is to run every deal both ways. This guide does that with two hypothetical houses and shows which numbers decide it.

## Key takeaways

- **Flip if values are high relative to rent,** you need the cash back, and you can sell into a healthy market.
- **BRRRR if rent is strong relative to value,** you can wait out a refinance, and you want to own rentals long term.
- **A flip returns all of your capital plus profit, minus taxes, at the sale.** A BRRRR returns some or all of your capital at the refinance and leaves you with debt, equity and a rental.
- **The same house can be a good flip and a bad BRRRR,** or the reverse.
- **BRRRR's biggest risks are the refinance and the cash flow.** A flip's biggest risks are time and the sale price.
- **Run both before you make an offer.** The analysis takes minutes.

## How to apply this

1. **Underwrite the purchase once:** ARV, rehab, all-in cost.
2. **Run it as a flip** in the [flip profit calculator](/tools/flip-profit-calculator): net profit, break-even sale price, cash needed, months.
3. **Run it as a BRRRR** in the [BRRRR calculator](/tools/brrrr-calculator): cash left after refinance, monthly cash flow after all expenses, DSCR.
4. **Check the refinance rules** with a lender before buying: LTV, DSCR minimum, seasoning.
5. **Decide with your capital in mind:** what you need back, when, and what you can afford to leave in.

## Inputs explained

| Question | Points toward flip | Points toward BRRRR |
|---|---|---|
| Rent relative to value | Low | High |
| What you need from this deal | Cash and profit soon | Long-term income and equity |
| Your available capital | Limited, must be recycled | Can tolerate some cash left in |
| Market for buyers | Strong retail demand | Weak sales, strong rental demand |
| Your appetite for landlording | Low | High, or you will pay a manager |
| Refinance availability | Uncertain | Confirmed lender, LTV and seasoning |
| Rehab type | Buyer-grade finishes that sell | Durable, rental-grade finishes |

## Formula

```
Flip:   Net profit        = Sale − (Purchase + Rehab + Holding + Financing + Buying and selling costs)
        Capital returned  = All your cash + Net profit (at the sale, before taxes)

BRRRR:  Cash left in deal = All-in cost + Refinance costs − New loan
        Capital recovery  = Cash returned at refinance ÷ Cash invested
        Monthly cash flow = Rent − Operating costs − Principal and interest
        Equity            = Appraised value − New loan
```

## Worked example (hypothetical): two houses, both ways

All numbers are hypothetical.

### House 1: FlipRadar's sample flip

1,500 sq ft ranch. Purchase $145,000, rehab $70,000, ARV $295,000, market rent $2,150.

| As a flip (six months) | | As a BRRRR (refinance at 75% LTV, 7.25%) | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net profit before tax | about $37,300 | New loan | $221,250 |
| Cash invested | about $43,800 | Cash left in deal | about $21,300 |
| Capital back at sale | all of it plus profit | Capital recovery | about 51% |
| Break-even sale price | about $254,900 | Lender-style DSCR | about 1.14 |
| | | Monthly cash flow (35% operating costs) | about −$110 |

**Verdict:** a good flip and a bad BRRRR. The rent is too low relative to the value to support the refinance debt. Lender-style DSCR falls below a common 1.20–1.25 rule of thumb, and the property loses money monthly even before full reserves.

### House 2: FlipRadar's sample rental

1,200 sq ft, 3 bed / 1.5 bath. Purchase $85,000, rehab $40,000, ARV $185,000, market rent $1,950.

| As a flip (six months) | | As a BRRRR (refinance at 75% LTV, 7.25%) | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net profit before tax | about $32,500 | New loan | $138,750 |
| Cash invested | about $27,300 | Cash left in deal | about $4,300 |
| Capital back at sale | all of it plus profit | Capital recovery | about 84% |
| | | Lender-style DSCR | about 1.56 |
| | | Monthly cash flow (all expenses incl. reserves) | about $138 |
| | | Equity after refinance | about $46,250 |

**Verdict:** both work. As a flip, it produces a solid profit for the price point. As a BRRRR, it returns most of the cash, keeps about $46,000 of equity, and passes the lender's test, but true cash flow is thin. The choice comes down to whether you want $32,500 of taxable profit now or a rental with a little cash left in and modest monthly income.

### The seasoning catch

The BRRRR numbers assume a lender will refinance at month six. Not every lender will. Under Fannie Mae's Selling Guide, for example, a cash-out refinance generally requires at least six months on title, and an existing first mortgage being paid off must be at least 12 months old (with exceptions such as delayed financing for cash purchases). A short-term rehab loan may not meet that. Many BRRRR investors use rental-focused (DSCR) lenders with their own seasoning rules. Confirm before you buy, because every extra month on a rehab loan costs interest.

## What the result means

- **Strong as a flip, weak as a BRRRR:** sell it.
- **Strong as a BRRRR, weak as a flip:** keep it, if the refinance is confirmed.
- **Strong both ways:** decide based on your capital and goals. Some investors flip to build cash, then BRRRR to build a portfolio.
- **Weak both ways:** the purchase price is wrong.

### What makes a good or bad deal for each

| | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|
| Flip | Bought well below ARV; clear scope; sells into active retail demand; enough cushion to survive a long hold | Thin margin that depends on the top comp; heavy unknown scope; slow market; expensive money |
| BRRRR | Rent supports debt with room to spare; refinance returns most cash; durable rehab; lender confirmed | Needs maximum LTV and a perfect appraisal; cash flow only works without reserves; no lender lined up |

### Pros and cons

| | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Flip | Capital and profit returned in months; no tenants; simpler exit; one loan | Profit usually taxed in the year of sale; exposed to sale price and time; must find the next deal to keep earning |
| BRRRR | Keep the asset and its income; can recycle most of the cash; long-term equity | Refinance and seasoning risk; thin cash flow at high leverage; landlord responsibilities; capital stuck if the appraisal is low |

## Assumptions

- Both houses appraise at their ARVs and rent at market.
- Financing terms and costs are as described; the refinance closes at month six.
- Operating costs include vacancy, management, maintenance and reserves where noted.
- No income taxes are modeled. How flip profit and rental income are taxed depends on your facts; see the [flip taxes guide](/guides/flip-taxes-short-term-gains-basics-not-tax-advice).

## Limitations

- Two houses do not represent a market.
- The BRRRR comparison ignores appreciation, rent growth, principal paydown and tax effects, which can matter over many years.
- Lender terms vary. Use current quotes.

## Common mistakes

1. **Deciding the strategy before running the numbers.**
2. **Forcing a high-value, low-rent house into a BRRRR.**
3. **Counting on a refinance that has not been confirmed.**
4. **Using lender DSCR as proof of cash flow.**
5. **Flip-grade finishes on a rental.**
6. **Forgetting that flip profit is taxed** and is not all available to reinvest.
7. **Leaving too much cash in a BRRRR** and calling it recycling.

## FAQ

### Is BRRRR better than flipping?

Neither is better in general. BRRRR fits houses with strong rent relative to value and investors who want long-term rentals. Flipping fits houses with high resale value relative to rent and investors who need capital back.

### Which needs more capital, a flip or a BRRRR?

The upfront cash can be similar. The difference is what happens after: a flip returns your cash at the sale, while a BRRRR returns only what the refinance gives back and may leave some in the deal.

### Can I start with flips and move to BRRRR?

Yes. Many investors flip to build cash and experience, then hold rentals. Run each deal both ways regardless.

### How long does a BRRRR refinance take?

It depends on rehab time, leasing, appraisal and the lender's seasoning rules. Some lenders require several months of ownership, and some conventional rules require the existing mortgage to be a year old. Confirm before you buy.

### What DSCR do lenders want for a BRRRR?

Around 1.20–1.25 is a common rule of thumb, but each lender sets its own minimum and definition. Passing DSCR does not guarantee positive cash flow after all expenses.

### What makes a house a good BRRRR?

Rent strong enough to support refinance debt with room to spare, a purchase price low enough that the refinance returns most of your cash, and a durable rehab.

### What makes a house a good flip?

A purchase price well below a conservative ARV, a clear scope, strong buyer demand, and enough cushion to survive a longer hold or a softer sale price.

### Should I analyze every deal both ways?

Yes. It takes a few minutes and sometimes reveals that a mediocre flip is a solid rental, or that a rental idea is really a flip.

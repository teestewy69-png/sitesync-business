Holding costs are what the house charges you for every day you own it. They start the day you close on the purchase and stop the day you close on the sale. Not the day the rehab finishes, and not the day you list.

Six months is a useful planning number. ATTOM's Q2 2026 U.S. Home Flipping Report puts the typical national time from purchase to resale at 161 days, a little over five months. Your hold depends on scope, permits and your market, so plan with your own estimate and test a longer one.

## Key takeaways

- **The holding period is closing to closing:** purchase closing, rehab, permits, listing, the buyer's contract period, sale closing.
- **Two kinds of holding cost:** operating costs (taxes, insurance, utilities, HOA, lawn, security) and financing costs (interest). Points and lender fees are one-time financing costs; count them once.
- **Interest is usually the biggest monthly line on a financed flip,** and on a draw-funded loan it rises as rehab money is released.
- **Every extra month costs the same monthly amount again,** plus any extension fee. Price a longer hold before you offer.
- **Holding costs belong in your max offer,** not in your rehab budget.

## How to apply this

1. **Estimate the hold by phase:** days to close, rehab weeks, permit and inspection time, days on market, buyer's closing period.
2. **List the operating costs** with real quotes: tax bill, insurance quote, utility estimates, HOA dues.
3. **Calculate financing interest** month by month if your loan funds rehab in draws. The [hard money loan calculator](/tools/hard-money-loan-calculator) shows how.
4. **Add it up for your base hold and a hold three months longer.**
5. **Put the total into your deal math** with the [flip profit calculator](/tools/flip-profit-calculator) and the [Maximum Purchase Price guide](/guides/maximum-purchase-price).

## Inputs explained

| Cost | What to know | Illustrative monthly range* |
|---|---|---|
| Property taxes | Prorated at purchase and sale; billed whether or not anyone lives there | Depends entirely on local rates and value |
| Insurance | Vacant or builder's risk coverage is often required and can cost more than a standard policy | A few hundred dollars |
| Utilities | Power, water, gas and trash during rehab and showings | A few hundred dollars |
| HOA dues | If applicable; ask about transfer fees and special assessments | $0 to several hundred |
| Lawn, snow and security | Required to keep the house presentable and protected | Under a few hundred dollars |
| Loan interest | Depends on rate, loan size, and whether interest is charged on drawn or full balance | Often the largest line |

*Illustrative only, not market data. Get local quotes. On FlipRadar's sample house, operating costs are a hypothetical $700 a month, and interest rises from about $1,130 to about $1,770 a month as draws fund.

## Formula

```
Holding period         = Purchase closing date → Sale closing date
Operating holding cost = Monthly operating costs × Months
Interest               = Σ (Outstanding loan balance each month × Annual rate ÷ 12)
Total holding cost     = Operating holding cost + Interest (+ extension fees)
Cost of one extra month ≈ Monthly operating costs + Full-balance monthly interest
```

## Worked example (hypothetical)

FlipRadar's sample flip: purchase $145,000, rehab $70,000, ARV $295,000. Hard money loan of 85% of purchase ($123,250) at closing plus $70,000 of rehab in four $17,500 draws, 11% interest charged on drawn funds. Operating costs $700 a month. All numbers are hypothetical.

| Month | Loan balance during the month | Interest | Operating | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $123,250 | $1,130 | $700 | $1,830 |
| 2 | $140,750 | $1,290 | $700 | $1,990 |
| 3 | $158,250 | $1,451 | $700 | $2,151 |
| 4 | $175,750 | $1,611 | $700 | $2,311 |
| 5 | $193,250 | $1,771 | $700 | $2,471 |
| 6 | $193,250 | $1,771 | $700 | $2,471 |
| **Total** | | **about $9,025** | **$4,200** | **about $13,225** |

Points ($3,865) and lender fees ($2,750) are paid once and are not monthly holding costs. Count them in financing, not here, so they are not counted twice.

**What one extra month costs:** about $2,471 at the full balance, plus any extension fee. If the hold runs to nine months with a one-point extension fee (about $1,933), holding and extension costs rise by about $9,300 and the sample deal's net profit falls from about $37,300 to about $28,000.

**How it changes your max offer:** each extra month you expect to hold lowers the most you can pay by about the same $2,471 if you want to keep the same profit. If your market's days on market are rising, lower your offer before you buy, not after.

## What the result means

- **Interest dominates on a leveraged flip.** Negotiating rate and interest method can matter more than shaving utilities.
- **Months matter more than line items.** A one-month delay usually costs more than any single operating line over the whole hold.
- **An all-cash flip still has holding costs,** and an opportunity cost on the cash.
- **If your holding total is larger than your contingency,** a two-month delay can wipe out the cushion you thought you had.

## Assumptions

- Operating costs are constant month to month.
- Draws fund at the end of months 1 through 4.
- No extension unless stated.
- Interest is charged only on funds drawn. If your lender charges on the full commitment, interest is higher.

## Limitations

- Taxes and insurance vary widely by location and property. The illustrative ranges are not market data.
- The example ignores prorations at closing and any prepaid items.
- National timing data describes all flips combined, not your scope or market.

## Common mistakes

1. **Starting the clock at rehab or ending it at listing.** It runs closing to closing.
2. **Leaving interest out of holding costs.**
3. **Counting points and fees as monthly costs,** then again as financing.
4. **Assuming standard homeowner's insurance covers a vacant rehab.** Ask your agent.
5. **Ignoring HOA special assessments and transfer fees.**
6. **Planning only the optimistic hold.**
7. **Shutting off utilities** in freezing weather or before inspections and showings.

## FAQ

### What are holding costs on a flip?

The costs of owning the house from purchase closing to sale closing: property taxes, insurance, utilities, HOA dues, lawn and security, and loan interest.

### How long does a typical flip take?

ATTOM's Q2 2026 report puts the typical national time from purchase to resale at 161 days. Your timeline depends on scope, permits and your market.

### Are loan points a holding cost?

They are a financing cost paid once, usually at closing. Count them once in your deal model, not as a monthly holding cost.

### Do I pay property taxes on a vacant flip?

Yes. Property taxes are based on ownership, not occupancy. Taxes are usually prorated at purchase and sale.

### What insurance do I need on a flip?

Usually a vacant property or builder's risk policy. Lenders often require specific coverage. Ask an insurance agent who works with investors.

### How do holding costs affect my maximum offer?

Every month of expected holding cost reduces the most you can pay for the same profit. Include holding costs in your Maximum Purchase Price calculation.

### Can I roll holding costs into my loan?

Some lenders allow interest reserves or finance certain costs. You then pay interest on a larger balance. Compare total cost both ways.

### What if my flip takes longer than six months?

Add the monthly holding cost for each extra month, plus any extension fee, and re-run your profit. Decide early whether to cut price or scope to sell sooner.

Most BRRRR analyses run forward: here is the price, here is the rehab, let's see what the refinance gives back. That order invites you to fall for the house first and justify the numbers second.

Run it backward instead. The refinance and the rent decide what the deal is worth to you. The purchase price is the last number you calculate, not the first one you accept.

This guide walks through that process with FlipRadar's hypothetical sample rental. For the calculator and the full formulas, see the [BRRRR calculator](/tools/brrrr-calculator).

## Key takeaways

- **Start with rent and ARV, not price.** Leased comps and a realistic appraisal set every other number.
- **Work out the refinance loan first,** then the most you can spend all-in, then the most you can pay for the house.
- **The lender's limit is usually LTV, not DSCR.** On the sample rental, the lender's DSCR test would support a much larger loan than its 75% LTV cap allows.
- **Your own cash flow test is usually the tightest.** Decide the minimum monthly cash flow you will accept and size the loan to that, not just to the lender's limit.
- **Confirm seasoning and the refinance lender before you buy.** A delayed refinance costs interest and holding costs every month.
- **Stress-test rent, appraisal, rate and timing.** A BRRRR that only works with everything going right is a bet, not a plan.

## How to apply: the seven-step analysis

1. **Rent.** Get leased comps for the finished house (not asking rents), or a property manager's written opinion.
2. **ARV.** Comps for the finished house, valued the way an appraiser would. See [how to estimate ARV without an agent](/guides/how-to-estimate-arv-without-an-agent).
3. **Refinance terms.** Get a real quote or term sheet: LTV, rate, closing costs, DSCR minimum, seasoning rules.
4. **Size the loan three ways** (LTV cap, lender DSCR, your cash flow target) and use the smallest.
5. **Work back to a maximum all-in cost and a maximum purchase price** for the amount of cash you are willing to leave in.
6. **Check seasoning against your timeline,** and price in the cost of a delay.
7. **Stress-test,** then decide: buy at or below your number, renegotiate, or walk.

## Inputs explained

| Input | Where it comes from | Common trap |
|---|---|---|
| Market rent | Leased comps, property manager | Asking rents or best-case rent |
| ARV | Sold comps, appraiser's view | Using a flip-grade ARV for a rental-grade rehab |
| Refinance LTV | Lender quote | Assuming the highest LTV advertised |
| Refinance rate and costs | Lender quote | Forgetting points and closing costs on the new loan |
| Lender DSCR minimum | Lender quote | Not asking how the lender defines DSCR |
| Operating costs | Taxes, insurance, vacancy, management, maintenance, reserves | Leaving out vacancy, maintenance or reserves |
| Your cash flow minimum | Your decision | Not setting one |
| All-in cost | Purchase, rehab, buying costs, rehab-loan costs, holding | Counting only purchase plus rehab |
| Seasoning | Lender guidelines | Learning the rule after closing |

## Formula

```
Loan (LTV cap)          = ARV × refinance LTV
Loan (lender DSCR)      = loan whose P&I = (Rent ÷ lender DSCR minimum) − monthly taxes and insurance
Loan (your cash flow)   = loan whose P&I = Rent − all operating costs − your minimum cash flow
Refinance loan          = smallest of the three

Max all-in cost         = Refinance loan − refinance closing costs + cash you will leave in
Cash left in deal       = All-in cost − (Refinance loan − refinance closing costs)
```

DSCR here follows the two definitions used on the [BRRRR calculator](/tools/brrrr-calculator) page: lender-style (rent ÷ principal, interest, taxes and insurance) and investor-style (net operating income ÷ principal and interest). Lenders differ, so ask yours.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

FlipRadar's sample rental. All numbers are hypothetical.

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---|
| Asking price | $85,000 |
| Rehab | $40,000 |
| ARV | $185,000 |
| Market rent | $1,950 a month |
| Taxes and insurance | $300 a month |
| Vacancy, management, maintenance, reserves | 29% of rent ($566) |
| Refinance | 75% LTV, 7.25%, 30 years, closing costs 2.5% of the loan |
| Lender DSCR minimum | 1.25 (hypothetical) |
| Your minimum cash flow | $200 a month (your choice) |

### Step 4: size the loan three ways

| Constraint | Maximum loan |
|---|---|
| LTV cap: $185,000 × 75% | **$138,750** |
| Lender DSCR at 1.25: P&I up to $1,560 − $300 = $1,260 | about $184,700 |
| Your cash flow of $200: P&I up to $1,950 − $866 − $200 = $884 | **about $129,600** |

The lender's DSCR test is not close to binding here; the LTV cap is. But your own cash flow target is tighter still. At the full $138,750 loan, principal and interest is about $947 and cash flow is about $138 a month, below the $200 you said you needed.

That is the blind spot in a lot of BRRRR analysis: "the lender approved it" only means the lender is covered.

### Step 5: work back to a purchase price

At the asking price, the all-in cost (purchase, rehab, buying costs, rehab-loan costs and six months of holding) is about $139,577. See the [BRRRR calculator](/tools/brrrr-calculator) page for the line items.

| Scenario | Refinance loan | Net of refi costs | Cash left in deal | Monthly cash flow |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full 75% loan, asking price $85,000 | $138,750 | $135,281 | about $4,300 | about $138 |
| Full 75% loan, purchase about $81,000 | $138,750 | $135,281 | $0 | about $138 |
| Loan sized to $200 cash flow, asking price | about $129,600 | about $126,350 | about $13,200 | about $200 |

To leave **zero** cash in with the full loan, the all-in cost must come down by about $4,300. Because buying costs, points and interest move with the price, each $1 off the purchase price saves about $1.08 of all-in cost in this example. That puts the purchase price for full capital recovery at about **$81,000**.

To get **$200 a month** of cash flow, you accept a smaller loan and leave about $13,200 in the deal at the asking price. Neither answer is wrong. You are choosing between recycling capital and having a cushion. Decide which one you need before you make the offer.

### Step 6: seasoning and timing

Under Fannie Mae's Selling Guide (B2-1.3-03), a cash-out refinance generally requires at least one borrower to have been on title for at least six months, and an existing first mortgage being paid off generally must be at least 12 months old. There are exceptions, including delayed financing for some cash purchases. Many BRRRR investors use non-agency rental (DSCR) loans with their own rules. Get the rule from the lender who will actually do the refinance, in writing, before you buy.

**Cost of a three-month delay (hypothetical):** interest on the $112,250 rehab loan at 11% (about $3,087) plus three more months of vacant holding costs at $550 (about $1,650) adds about $4,700. Cash left in the deal roughly doubles to about $9,000, before any extension fee the rehab lender charges.

### Step 7: stress tests

| Scenario | Cash left in deal | Monthly cash flow | Lender DSCR | Investor DSCR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | about $4,300 | about $138 | 1.56 | 1.14 |
| Rent $1,800 instead of $1,950 | about $4,300 | about $31 | 1.44 | 1.03 |
| Rate 8.00% | about $4,300 | about $66 | 1.48 | 1.06 |
| Appraisal $170,000 | about $15,300 | about $215 | 1.67 | 1.25 |
| Refinance three months late | about $9,000 | about $138 | 1.56 | 1.14 |

The rent stress is the one that hurts: a $150 miss leaves about $31 a month of cash flow, while the lender's test still passes comfortably. A low appraisal leaves more money in but improves cash flow, because the loan is smaller.

**Verdict on the sample:** a workable BRRRR at or below about $81,000 if full capital recovery is the goal and you accept thin cash flow; or at the asking price if you accept leaving about $13,000 in for a $200 cushion. Paying the asking price and taking the full loan gets you neither.

## What the result means

- **All three loan sizes agree:** the deal fits the lender and your cash flow at the same time. Rare, and worth moving on.
- **Your cash flow limit is lowest:** normal. Choose between leaving cash in and accepting thinner cash flow, or negotiate the price down.
- **Lender DSCR is lowest:** rent is weak relative to value. The deal may be a better flip than a rental. See [BRRRR vs flip](/guides/brrrr-vs-flip-which-fits-your-capital).
- **Cash left is large at any reasonable price:** treat it as a regular rental purchase, or pass.

## Assumptions

- Operating costs scale with rent except taxes and insurance, which are fixed.
- 30-year fixed, fully amortizing refinance.
- Rehab loan and holding costs as in the [BRRRR calculator](/tools/brrrr-calculator) example.
- Lender terms are hypothetical. Real terms vary by lender, borrower and property.

## Limitations

- No appreciation, rent growth, principal paydown or tax effects.
- Lender DSCR definitions vary; some include HOA dues or use a different rent figure.
- Seasoning rules vary by loan program and change over time. Read the current guide.
- Not financing or tax advice. Ask a CPA how refinance proceeds and rental income affect your taxes.

## Common mistakes

1. **Accepting the asking price and analyzing forward.**
2. **Treating lender approval as proof the deal cash flows.**
3. **Assuming the maximum LTV and a full appraisal.**
4. **Leaving rehab-loan and holding costs out of all-in cost.**
5. **Not getting seasoning rules in writing before closing.**
6. **Stress-testing the appraisal but not the rent.** Rent misses hit cash flow directly.
7. **No minimum cash flow.** Without one, every deal with a positive number looks acceptable.

## FAQ

### What is the first number to check on a BRRRR deal?

Market rent from leased comps, followed by ARV. Both decide the refinance and the cash flow; the purchase price comes last.

### How do I calculate my maximum purchase price for a BRRRR?

Find the refinance loan (the smallest of your LTV, lender DSCR and cash flow limits), subtract refinance costs, add the cash you are willing to leave in, then subtract rehab, buying, holding and rehab-loan costs.

### What DSCR do lenders require?

It varies by lender and program. Ask for the minimum and how they calculate it. Then run your own investor-style DSCR, which includes vacancy, management, maintenance and reserves.

### Why does my lender approve a deal that barely cash flows?

The lender's DSCR usually compares rent to the payment with taxes and insurance, not to all your costs. It protects the lender, not your cash flow.

### What is seasoning in a BRRRR?

The time a lender requires you to own the property, or hold the existing loan, before a refinance. Fannie Mae's guide generally requires six months on title for a cash-out refinance, with exceptions. Other lenders set their own rules.

### Is it better to pull all my cash out or keep cash flow?

It depends on your capital and goals. Full recovery lets you buy again sooner but leaves thinner cash flow. Leaving some cash in buys a cushion. The worked example shows both.

### What if the appraisal comes in low?

You get a smaller loan and leave more cash in, but cash flow improves. Plan for it by underwriting a conservative ARV.

### How much should I budget for a refinance delay?

Rehab-loan interest plus monthly holding costs for each month of delay, plus any extension fee. In the example, three months adds about $4,700.
